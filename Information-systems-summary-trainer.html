Millions could be working a four-day week by 2033 thanks to AI

By Luke Hurst, Euronews.com, November 11, 2023

Incorporating artificial intelligence (AI) into workflows could mean we work less for the same pay, according to the think tank Autonomy.

Nearly a third of the UK's workforce could be working a four-day week by 2033 thanks to the rollout of advanced artificial intelligence (AI) technology, a new report claims.

One of the biggest concerns around the increasing power and popularity of AI tech over the past couple of years is whether it will take jobs away from humans, as it is proven to be capable of taking on more of the tasks that humans currently perform.

A recent survey from LinkedIn found nearly half of European workers are expecting AI to make a major impact on their working lives within the next year.

Generative text AI tools like Open Ai's ChatGPT and Google's Bard can write whole articles or business plans in seconds, producing a close approximation of what a human can do when fed the right prompts and information.

Image generation tools meanwhile can conjure up photorealistic pictures nearly instantaneously, raising questions about job security for human artists and illustrators.

But the new report from Autonomy, an independent think tank that looks at the future of work and economic planning, sets out a more promising vision of AI's impact on our working lives.

In its GPT-4 report, the authors suggest 8.8 million workers in the UK - 28 per cent of the workforce - could be working 32-hour, four-day weeks thanks to AI.

A further 27.9 million meanwhile - representing 88 per cent of the country's workforce - could have working hours reduced by at least 10 per cent too.

AI could usher in new work benefits.

Autonomy made its calculations using the 1.5 per cent annual productivity increase from AI that was estimated by Goldman Sachs in a recent study on the topic of AI and GDP, as well as data from the US Department of Labor's Occupational Information Network (O*NET).

The Autonomy report outlines two forecasts for leveraging AI to boost productivity while delivering better conditions for workers, specifically looking at how the ideas could be rolled out in local authorities in the UK.

The two scenarios are a 20 per cent reduction in working hours while keeping the same pay for workers, or one in which worker productivity increases by 10 per cent, enabling a 10 per cent reduction in working hours, while keeping the same pay.

"Our research offers a fresh perspective in debates around how AI can be utilised for good," said Will Stronge, director of research at Autonomy.

"A shorter working week is the most tangible way of ensuring that AI delivers benefits to workers as well as companies. If AI is to be implemented fairly across the economy, it should usher in a new era of four-day working weeks for all".

Various four-day week trials are either underway or have been recently completed, with the majority of results pointing towards either maintained or improved levels of productivity, with substantial benefits for worker well-being.

02

Reformulate & Order

Un résumé reformule sans recopier. Reformulez deux phrases, puis remettez les idées clés dans l'ordre logique d'un résumé.

Reformulate the following sentence in your own words.
"A shorter working week is the most tangible way of ensuring that AI delivers benefits to workers as well as companies."
Modèle : Reducing working hours is the clearest, most concrete way to make sure that both employees and businesses gain from AI-driven productivity.
Reformulate the following sentence in your own words.
"In its GPT-4 report, the authors suggest 8.8 million workers in the UK - 28 per cent of the workforce - could be working 32-hour, four-day weeks thanks to AI."
Modèle : According to the report, AI could enable close to 9 million UK employees, more than a quarter of the workforce, to switch to a 32-hour week spread over four days.
Put these key ideas in the logical order of a summary.Utilisez les menus pour numéroter de 1 à 5.

AI's growing power raises concerns about whether it will take jobs away from humans.

The calculations rely on productivity data from Goldman Sachs and the US Department of Labor.
Ongoing and completed four-day week trials already point to steady or improved productivity.

Summary Conventions

Quelques règles à respecter avant de rédiger. Vérifiez votre compréhension des conventions du résumé.

In a DSCG summary, you should…
Aadd your own personal opinions and develop several detailed examples from your own experience.
Breformulate the author's ideas in your own words, respecting the logical order and word limit.
Ccopy the most important sentences of the text word for word, in their original order.
Dtranslate the entire article into French as faithfully and completely as you possibly can.

Which register is appropriate for a summary?
AA first-person style that openly expresses the writer's own personal opinion throughout.
BA neutral, objective tone written in the third person, with no long direct quotations.
CA series of direct quotations taken from the text and linked together with connectors.
DAn informal, conversational tone, as if you were simply chatting with a close friend.

Résumé modèle

The article reports on a study by the think tank Autonomy suggesting that AI could allow millions of UK workers to move to a four-day week. While AI's growing power raises widespread concern about job losses, with nearly half of European workers expecting a major impact within a year, Autonomy offers a more optimistic outlook. Its GPT-4 report estimates that 28% of the UK workforce, around 8.8 million people, could work 32-hour, four-day weeks by 2033, while a further 88% could see their hours cut by at least 10%, with pay unchanged. These figures are based on productivity data from Goldman Sachs and the US Department of Labor. Autonomy's director of research argues that a shorter week is the clearest way to share AI's benefits fairly between workers and companies. The article notes that ongoing and completed four-day week trials already show steady or improved productivity, along with real gains for employee wellbeing.

Points clés (support oral)

  • Subject — Autonomy report: AI could enable a four-day week for millions of UK workers.
  • Context — AI raises job-loss fears; nearly half of European workers expect a major impact within a year.
  • Key figures — 28% (8.8 million) could work 32-hour weeks by 2033; 88% could see hours cut by 10%.
  • Data used — Goldman Sachs productivity estimate (1.5%/year) + US Department of Labor (O*NET) data.
  • Argument — a shorter week is the most tangible way to share AI's benefits fairly (Will Stronge).
  • Evidence — ongoing/completed four-day week trials show steady or improved productivity and wellbeing gains.
